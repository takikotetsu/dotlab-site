    Where does "complete" actually begin?

    1. 1

      Written

      The OS accepted the write. The progress bar is gone, but the data may still be sitting in memory.

    2. 2

      Verified

      Read back from the destination and hash compared. If that read is served from cache, it only compared memory against memory.

    3. 3

      Flushed

      Read with the cache bypassed, then forced down to the disk itself. Only now does the footage survive a power loss.

    Most tools say "complete" at 2. .Lab goes to 3.

    Features

    ● Your originals are untouchable

    Source media is opened read-only, by design. Overwriting or deleting originals is structurally impossible. Until your backup is verified, your originals stay exactly as they were.

    ● Power loss? Resume where you left off

    A power cut, a yanked card, a frozen reader — .Lab notices within seconds and stops safely. Every step is journaled, so it resumes right where it stopped, skipping verified files. Half-written files never masquerade as finished ones.

    ● Two backups in one pass

    Read the card once, write to multiple SSDs simultaneously (Pro). Chain copies for backups-of-backups, plus a watch-folder mode that copies new files as they appear.

    ● Proof, not promises

    Export verification results as a thumbnail PDF report or industry-standard ASC MHL (Pro). Hand clients evidence — and answer “did it really copy?” with receipts.

    ● Insert card, ready to go

    Auto-detects Sony, Canon, RED, ARRI and Blackmagic cards as well as regular SD cards (DCIM). Insert a card and it’s set as the source, reel name suggested automatically.

    ● Check your footage on the spot

    Double-click a finished reel to browse thumbnails, play clips and inspect metadata (for formats macOS can play). Get completion alerts by chime, macOS notification — or email when you’re away from the cart.

    ● No slowdown on RAW sequences

    Thousands of tiny frames — CinemaDNG, ARRIRAW — are where most copy tools grind to a crawl. .Lab batches the commit work under the hood, keeping full verification depth while holding its speed on sequences.

    Then keep it backed up.

    The offload ends; the day doesn’t. Timelines, exports, notes, stills — the project keeps growing after the cards are in. Sync mirrors a folder to a second drive and keeps mirroring it, copying only what changed. Not just camera originals — everything you make.

    There ten seconds after you save it

    Sync listens to the OS for changes, so nothing waits on a timer. It also waits for writing to stop before it copies, so a file still being saved never gets copied half-finished.

    Any number of pairs, one window

    Run A→C and B→D at once and see which is working, which is waiting, and how long until the next sweep. Close the app and reopen it — each pair catches up from where it stopped.

    Nothing gets deleted until you press start

    Choose “mirror exactly” and files missing from the source are removed from the destination too. That never begins on its own — not on launch, not on reopen. You press start.

    FAQ

    Can .Lab damage or delete my original files?
    No. Source media is opened strictly read-only, and no code path exists that writes to or deletes from it. It’s a design principle, continuously enforced by the test suite.
    What if power dies mid-copy?
    All state is committed to an SQLite journal as it happens. On relaunch, .Lab detects the interrupted job and resumes, skipping verified files. Partially written files never end up under their final name (atomic commits).
    How do I use it?
    Drag a card or folder onto SOURCE (inserted cards are detected automatically), pick a destination, press Start Offload. That's it — a confirmation appears when everything is verified. Full usage notes and FAQ are built into the app (⌘?) and work offline.
    What does the destination structure look like?
    destination / reel / media name / (card contents as-is), preserving the card's own hierarchy the way sets expect. Leave the reel name empty to copy straight into the destination.
    If I stop a copy, do I start over?
    No. Verified files are recorded, so resuming continues where it left off. Partially written files never take their final name.
    I have a half-finished Finder copy. Can .Lab finish it?
    Yes — turn on Repair Copy. Existing files are verified against the source one by one, good ones are kept, and only what's missing is copied. Corrupted files are reported in red, never deleted or overwritten.
    What if a sequence has missing frames?
    You're warned before copying, and the report records that those frames were already absent on the card — proof it wasn't lost in transfer.
    Two Macs?
    One license covers two Macs at a time. Deactivate in Settings to move it to another machine.
    Does the free version verify too?
    Yes — copy and verification are identical to Pro in quality and speed. Free is limited to one destination, one job at a time and one project; queue, watch folder, report PDFs and more are Pro.
    Which cameras and formats are supported?
    Copying and verification are format-agnostic — every file type works. Card auto-detection covers Sony, Canon, RED, ARRI, Blackmagic and still-photo cards (DCIM). Shooting metadata is read from Sony sidecar files (XAVC/X-OCN); RAW formats (REDCODE/ARRIRAW/BRAW) show the format name only. Thumbnails and playback are available for formats macOS can natively play (MP4/MOV/ProRes, JPEG/HEIF, etc.).
    Can I use it offline on location?
    Yes. Copying and verification are fully local, and your license never expires from being offline — no matter how long. It is only deactivated if the server explicitly refuses it, such as after a refund.
